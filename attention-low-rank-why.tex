\begin{center}
\textit{LayerNorm subtracts the mean. That's it.}
\end{center}

\section*{The Claim}

Transformers apply LayerNorm before attention. LayerNorm subtracts the mean, forcing all vectors onto a $(d{-}1)$-dimensional hyperplane. Since $Q$ and $K$ are linear projections of these vectors, $\textbf{rank}(QK^\top) \leq d{-}1$.

\section*{The Proof (2D $\to$ 1D)}

Take $\mathbf{x} = [x_1, x_2]$. Subtract the mean $\mu = (x_1 + x_2)/2$. The result $\mathbf{z} = [x_1 - \mu,\; x_2 - \mu]$ satisfies:
%
\[
z_1 + z_2 \;=\; (x_1 - \mu) + (x_2 - \mu) \;=\; x_1 + x_2 - 2\mu \;=\; 0
\]
%
So $z_2 = -z_1$. Every centered vector is $\mathbf{z} = [a, -a] = a \cdot [1, -1]$. Stack $N$ such vectors:
%
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
1.2 & -1.2 \\
-0.8 & 0.8 \\
2.0 & -2.0 \\
0.5 & -0.5
\end{bmatrix}
\;=\;
\begin{bmatrix}
1.2 \\
-0.8 \\
2.0 \\
0.5
\end{bmatrix}
\times
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & -1
\end{bmatrix}
\]
%
Outer product: $(4 \times 1) \times (1 \times 2) \to (4 \times 2)$ matrix. \textbf{Rank = 1}, no matter how many rows.

\section*{Generalization to $d$ Dimensions}

In $d$ dimensions: $z_1 + z_2 + \cdots + z_d = 0$. One constraint on $d$ variables $\to$ $(d{-}1)$-dimensional subspace. The $[1,1,\ldots,1]$ direction is gone. Any matrix of such vectors has rank $\leq d{-}1$. Linear projections $Q = W_q \mathbf{z}$ and $K = W_k \mathbf{z}$ cannot increase rank, so $\textbf{rank}(QK^\top) \leq d{-}1$.

\section*{Verification}

Any vector decomposes as: $\;\mathbf{x} = \mu \cdot [1,1,\ldots,1] + (\mathbf{x} - \mu)$

First term: rank-1. Second term: LayerNorm output ($d{-}1$ dimensions). Adding one rank-1 component reconstructs the original. That component is exactly what LayerNorm deletes. \hfill $\blacksquare$
